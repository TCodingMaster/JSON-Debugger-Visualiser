Bar Replay, step by step

  1. Open a chart of MNQ1! or MGC1! (continuous front month) on the timeframe your concept uses (e.g. 1m/5m).
  2. Mark your context levels first: prior-day high/low, overnight high/low, session open.
  3. Click Replay in the top toolbar, pick a start date, and hide future bars. Step forward one bar at a time (Shift+→) — never skip ahead.
  4. When the setup appears (sweep → rejection block → FVG respected), place entry, stop, and a fixed 2R target with the Long/Short Position tool before the next bar.
  5. Step until stop or target is hit (or session end), then log the trade below with the honest checklist ticks.
  6. Repeat across many different days and regimes; aim for 50+ trades per concept before trusting the stats.

Methodology & caveats

How the Easy Score is built

    • Backtest rewards positive expectancy (R per trade) and profit factor on the best symbol. Robustness checks that out-of-sample expectancy stays positive, that win rate is stable between in-sample and out-of-sample, and that the concept works on both MNQ and MGC.
    • Fit = how closely the concept matches your playbook (liquidity sweep + rejection block + FVG). Credibility = evidence the source is a practising professional, minus guru red flags. Ease = how mechanical and simple the rules are.
    • Total = Σ(weight × component) / Σ(weights of available components). Grades: A ≥ 80, B ≥ 65, C ≥ 50, D ≥ 35, otherwise F.
    • Provisional scores (fewer than 10 trades) rely only on fit, credibility and ease and are capped at 45 (grade D at best), so an untested idea can never outrank a tested one.
    • Tested concepts with zero or negative expectancy on their best instrument are capped at 40, so they always rank below untested ideas.

    Data caveats

    • Backtests are bar-based. When stop and target both fall inside one bar, the lab assumes the stop was hit first (conservative). Costs: commission per contract per side, plus 1 tick of slippage on stop and market fills. Continuous-contract rolls can distort levels.
    • Discretionary concepts are approximated by code; the coded version is not the trader's exact method.
    • Small samples (under ~100 trades) give wide uncertainty — a 60% observed win rate on 40 trades is consistent with a true rate well below 50%.
    • Numbers are rounded for display; extra decimals would be false precision.
